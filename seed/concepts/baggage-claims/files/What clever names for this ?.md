# What clever names for this ?

Naming is pure wordplay territory, so let's have fun. Quick note: "Baggage Claims" is already strong (pun + travel-native), so treat these as challengers and tier options — and run a USPTO + domain screen before falling in love with any of them.

## Top contenders

- **Baggage Claims** — the incumbent. Legal-world pun, instantly says "luggage with complaints," and "file a claim" doubles as a return-policy joke. Hard to beat.
- **Sass Case** — "suitcase" with the attitude baked in. Two syllables, giftable, and the voice packs (Family / Salty / New Yorker) live inside the name.
- **Emotional Baggage** — the obvious one, but obvious works for gag gifts. Tagline writes itself: *"Now available as literal luggage."* Downside: hard to trademark cleanly, probably contested.
- **LoudMouth Luggage** — alliterative, retail-shelf friendly, reads great on an airport gift-shop counter display.
- **Checked \& Offended** — the checked-bag journey plus the personality. Slightly long, but it's the funniest bumper sticker of the bunch.


## Strong alternates

- **Bellyaching** — cargo "belly" + complaining. Deep cut, but rewarding.
- **The Mouthful** — plays on "a mouthful of luggage"… okay, this one's a stretch, skip it.
- **HeavyBag** — deadpan, airline-scale joke embedded (overweight fees), short and trademarkable.
- **Unclaimed** — carousel-native. Dark humor angle: the bag that refuses to shut up until somebody claims it.
- **Freightened** — freight + frightened. Cute, but reads better in print than out loud.


## Tier-able name system

Whatever wins, name the voice packs as a ladder — it makes the DLC strategy feel like a product line, not an accessory:

- **Coach** (Family Friendly) → **Premium Sass** (Salty) → **First Class** (New Yorker) — and the \$49 collector edition becomes **Black Card: Checked \& Offended Edition**.

My honest ranking: **Sass Case** edges out Baggage Claims for retail (says it in two syllables, survives being shouted across a store), while **Baggage Claims** wins for the Kickstarter video and PR angle ("the world's sassiest suitcase files its first claim"). If you're torn, do what gag brands often do — product name **Sass Case**, product line **Baggage Claims**, and the tag covers both: *"FRAGILE — and emotionally unstable."*

One caveat worth 15 minutes: run each finalist through the USPTO TESS database and a domain check before printing anything — "Emotional Baggage" especially is the kind of phrase that attracts pre-existing novelty trademarks.
