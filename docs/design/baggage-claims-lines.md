# Baggage Claims MK1: the lines

For the owner to star or strike. Every line has an id. Reply with ids, for example `star IMP-01 WGH-03, strike CAR-08`; a line you do not mention stays as a proposal. `Q5 a` in the short list takes the three marked best for each moment.

- **owner's**: your own words, kept exactly as you wrote them in the first idea note.
- **owner's documents**: the lines in your documents (the ranked catalogue, the launch video script, the design package), kept as written, each with its document. Where two documents word one line differently, both are listed and you pick.
- **proposed**: written on 22 Nov 2026 for this list, best first. ★ marks the three recommended for each sensed moment.

## The voice, and the rules every line keeps

A put-upon, dramatic suitcase: deadpan, sorry for itself, vain about its weight and its wheels, thrilled by the carousel, lonely in the hold. It complains to whoever is in earshot and never about them.

1. The bag is the butt of every joke. It is put-upon, dramatic, vain and sorry for itself; it is never mean to the people nearby, and it never comments on a person's body, age, job, accent or anything else about them.
2. Nothing about security, ever: no weapons, bombs, hijacking, smuggling, customs, drugs, fire or danger, no "what's in me" jokes, nothing about being unattended, tampered with or packed by someone else. Nothing an airport could treat as a threat or a false alarm.
3. No sounds that imitate an alarm, a siren, a scanner or an announcement, and no cry for help that could be taken for a person in trouble. The scream is a cartoon scream.
4. Nothing built on a stereotype of any group of people. A character voice is a character, not a nationality.
5. No celebrity impressions (the documents' own rule), and no named brands in a line without checking first.
6. Short. Most lines are under ten words, because they come out of a tiny speaker in a noisy hall. One idea a line.
7. Clips for the dark hold are recorded quiet.

Running gags across one trip, so the bag seems to remember:

- **The score.** The bag counts what is done to it: "That's one. I'm keeping count." on the first impact, "That's two. The list grows." on the second, "Adding it to the list." on the belt, "Not that I'm counting." on the carousel, and "Final score: too many bumps. One star." at the end. Needs from the firmware: a count of impacts in the journey and a clip for each of the first few.
- **The laps.** The carousel lines count: lap two, third lap.
- **The souvenirs and the scale.** The weight jokes come back in the dark ("Very slimming.") and after an impact ("Right on the souvenirs.").
- **The claim.** "I'd like to file a claim." says the product's name without an advert.
- **Are we there yet?** Asked on the belt, whispered in the hold.

One fact changes a line in the script: the unit takes two AA cells, so the host's "Three double-A batteries" becomes two.

The lines use American words (trunk, elevator, line), for a first market in the United States (Q4).

## What the lines ask of the firmware (for WS14)

1. A count of impacts within one journey, with clips for the first, the second and "too many".
2. A count of carousel laps, with a clip for the second and the third.
3. Clips for the dark hold played at a lower volume than the rest.
4. Several clips for one moment, chosen so the same one is not played twice running.
5. A goodbye on mute: the slide switch cuts the power, so a line on switching off needs a soft power-off or plays on mute only.
6. A first-start flag, so the out-of-the-box line plays once.
7. Clip numbers kept apart for moments added later, so an older pack still works (see the voice pack evaluation).
